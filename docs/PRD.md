## Executive Summary

New College students design their own majors. Every term they must decide which of roughly
9,600 offered sections are worth taking, against a requirement stack of five concurrent systems
— depth study, New College seminars, three gen-ed pools, two university designations, and hour
totals. DegreeWorks tells them what they still need (**F-07**, **F-19**). The catalog tells them
what is offered (**F-24**). **Nothing connects the two.** The mapping from a course to the
requirements it would close exists only in an advisor's head, one course at a time (**F-04**).

The cost is three to six hours or more per term, spread across weeks (**F-23**), and it is not
recovered. Both students who report missing a relevant course did not take it (**F-12**). One
narrowed her depth study because she could not find courses for it (**F-13**).

The cost is also invisible to the person paying it. One participant reported her difficulty as
"None" while reporting both of the above (**F-22**). Three simultaneously agree that they know
what is available and that there are courses they are unaware of (**F-28**). This governs the
product: it cannot be sold on felt frustration (**D-14**), and the success metric is missed
courses avoided (**D-12**), not time saved (**D-18**, secondary).

Students who turn to general AI tools get a specific, reproducible failure: it recommends
courses that are not offered (**F-26**, both AI users). Grounding every recommendation in the
real offered list is therefore the differentiator (**D-16**), not a safety afterthought.

### What Makes This Special

Three things, in order of how hard they are to copy:

1. **It answers the inverse question.** Every existing tool answers "what do I still need?"
   This answers "what would *this course* close?" — which is the question actually being asked
   (**F-15**, verbatim) and the one no system answers.
2. **It is grounded by construction.** The ranker may reorder real sections and may not invent
   one. This is enforced in code and tested, not promised in a prompt (**D-16**, **F-26**).
3. **It states what it does not know.** A rotating-topic course is shown, never hidden, with the
   unknown part named (**D-21**, **D-27**). Uncertainty attaches to topical fit and never to
   requirement closure, because the buckets are always trustworthy even when the topic is not.

## Success Criteria

### User Success

- A student can tell, for any offered section, which of *their* outstanding requirements it
  would close — without asking anyone (**F-15**, **F-04**).
- A student stops discovering relevant courses too late to take them (**F-12**).
- A student does not narrow their depth study for lack of findable courses (**F-13**).
- An advisor confirms a proposed term in one pass rather than course by course (**F-04**).

### Business Success

- New College gets a consistent advising artefact across advisors. Practice currently varies by
  advisor, which is both the pain and the adoption risk (**A-11**, **F-39**).
- The pre-advising checklist stops being retyped by hand each term (**F-35**).

### Technical Success

- Every recommended section is a real section offered in the term (**D-16**). Zero invented
  courses, enforced in code.
- Requirement tagging is deterministic and auditable. A student can see why a course was tagged
  (SOW §6 row 8).
- The product never depends on a network round trip to function (NFR5).

### Measurable Outcomes

| Metric | Baseline | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevant courses missed per student per term | ≥1 for 2 of 7 participants | 0 | **F-12**, **D-12** |
| Hours spent planning a term | 3–6+, spread over weeks | <1, one session | **F-23**, **D-18** |
| Sections recommended that are not offered | 2 of 2 AI users hit this | **0** | **F-26**, **D-16** |
| Requirements a student cannot map a course to | all of them | 0, or explicitly flagged unknown | **F-15**, **D-27** |

The third row is the one that must be zero rather than low. It is the failure the product
exists to prevent and the only metric with a hard floor.

## Product Scope

### MVP — Minimum Viable Product

One term, one institution, two roles. Requirement tagging, outstanding-requirement tracking,
phrase-driven ranking grounded in the real offered list, double-count surfacing, the advisor
views, the uncertainty flags and seminar themes. Covers FR1–FR34.

### Growth Features (Post-MVP)

- DegreeWorks audit import, replacing seeded coursework (**D-24**; raises a FERPA question)
- Multi-term planning — currently one term at a time is the demonstrated unit of work
- Per-course depth-study flags, which would make the depth-study hour rules exact rather than
  approximate
- Saved plans and advisor annotations

### Vision (Future)

- The requirement rule set versioned per catalog year, so a student on the 2024–25 catalog sees
  2024–25 rules (**D-29** and **D-32** are the first steps toward this)
- Extension to other self-designed-major programs at other institutions

### Explicitly out of scope

| Not building | Why |
|---|---|
| Automatic registration | Requires university systems we do not have |
| Replacing DegreeWorks | Trusted and working (**F-07**, **F-19**); this complements it (**D-13**) |
| Official approval of depth studies | Stays with the advisor and the third-year review (**D-26**) |
| Students in declared linear majors | Already served (**F-07**, **F-08**, **F-09**, **D-09**) |
| New College recruitment / awareness tooling | Real finding (**F-10**, **F-11**), different product, different users. Reported, not built. |
| Instructor quality or course reviews | Students get this from peers and forums (**F-06**, **F-18**) and are not asking us |
| An approval workflow entity | No formal approval exists before the third-year review (**D-26**) |

## User Journeys

### J1 — Student planning a term (primary)

Maya is a junior with a self-designed depth study in environmental policy. She has 6 hours of
Humanities outstanding including a required Literature course, and 4 hours of Natural Science
including her lab.

She opens the Navigator, sees her outstanding requirements with hours remaining, and types
`environmental policy`. The results show offered sections ranked so that anything closing one of
her outstanding requirements *and* matching her phrase comes first. `NEW 215 Perspectives on
Environmental Literature` appears at the top: it carries `HU`, it is a Literature course, it
closes a must-include constraint, and it matches her phrase. The card says which requirement it
closes, why it matched, and that the rule comes from the Pre-Advising Checklist rev. Nov 2025.

Further down, a special-topics section appears with its topic marked unknown and a note to ask
her advisor. It is not hidden, and the requirement it closes is still stated plainly.

### J2 — Advisor reviewing a term (primary)

Dr. Chen opens her advisee list and sees each student with a count of outstanding requirements.
She opens Maya, sees the same view Maya sees, and checks Maya's proposed term against every
outstanding requirement in one pass instead of course by course. Where the system proposes a
depth-study candidate, it is labelled *proposed* and she confirms or rejects it. The judgment
stays hers (**F-04**).

### J3 — Student who does not know they have the problem (**A-10**)

Sam reports no difficulty planning. He is not looking for a tool. He opens the Navigator because
his advisor sent the link, types his interest, and sees two courses closing requirements he had
not connected to his interests. The product has to work for a user who does not believe he needs
it — which is why it informs the manual search rather than replacing it (**D-04**, **F-02**), and
why it is never pitched on frustration (**D-14**).

## Functional Requirements

### Requirement mapping

- FR1: A student can see, for every section offered in the term, which of their outstanding requirements it would close. (**F-15**, **F-21**, **D-02**)
- FR2: A student can see why a section was tagged as it was — which Banner attribute or schedule type produced which requirement bucket. (SOW §6 row 8, **F-21**)
- FR3: The system displays the source document, its revision date, and the catalog year for the requirement rules in force. (**D-29**, **D-32**; `nc-website-audit.md`)
- FR4: A student can see which requirements a section closes even when the section's topic cannot be determined. (**D-27**)

### Outstanding requirements and progress

- FR5: A student can see each outstanding requirement with hours remaining and hours completed. (**F-19**, **F-16**)
- FR6: The system shows must-include constraints distinctly from hour totals — "9 hours, including at least one Literature". (**D-28**, Pre-Advising Checklist)
- FR7: The system computes a requirement as a union of gen-ed buckets, not a hierarchy, and never counts one completed course twice against the same requirement. (**D-28**)
- FR8: A student's completed coursework is loaded into the system and drives what counts as outstanding. (**D-24**, **F-19**)

### Discovery and ranking

- FR9: A student or advisor can enter a free-text interest phrase; no fixed taxonomy of topics or careers exists. (**D-22**, resolves **Q-24**)
- FR10: The system ranks only sections actually offered in the modelled term. (**D-16**, **F-26**)
- FR11: Sections that close an outstanding requirement **and** match the phrase rank above sections doing either alone. (**D-19**, **F-41**)
- FR12: Sections closing two or more outstanding requirements are surfaced above sections closing one. (**D-11**, **F-14**, **A-05**)
- FR13: Results are deduplicated by course, with the number of additional sections stated. (prototype measurement: up to 30 rows per course)
- FR14: A student can browse the full offering list with requirement tags shown and no phrase entered. (**D-04**, **F-01**, **F-02**)
- FR15: A student is told when their phrase matched nothing, rather than being shown an arbitrary ranking. (**D-17**)

### AI reranking — the core AI feature

- FR16: A second-stage reranker reorders the retrieved candidate set by relevance to the phrase. (**D-22**, **A-04**)
- FR17: The reranker may only reorder sections it was given. It cannot introduce a section. (**D-16**, **F-26**)
- FR18: Every section identifier the reranker returns is validated against the input set; anything not in it is discarded and the discarded count is reported. (**D-16**)
- FR19: A candidate the reranker omits from its ordering is appended rather than dropped. (**D-16**)
- FR20: The reranker produces a one-line reason for each result, derived from the course text it was given. (SOW §6 row 8)
- FR21: Any reranker failure falls back to the deterministic ranking and serves results rather than erroring. (NFR5)
- FR22: Only the interest phrase, the names of outstanding requirement buckets, and public catalog text are sent off the machine. No name, student ID, completed coursework, or grades. (NFR12)

### Uncertainty and refusal

- FR23: A section whose topic cannot be determined from catalog text is shown, never hidden, and labelled "cannot determine the topic — ask your advisor". (**D-21** as refined by **D-27**)
- FR24: Topical uncertainty never attaches to requirement closure. A section's gen-ed buckets remain stated plainly even when its topic is unknown. (**D-27**)
- FR25: A section is flagged uncertain only when it has a special-topics section **and** its description carries no topical signal — not on the special-topics attribute alone. (**D-27**; 136 Legends sections carry the attribute and are a known requirement)

### New College seminars

- FR26: A NEW course is shown with its seminar theme — CC, ESC or SPSC — for the modelled term. (**D-31**, partly reversing **D-23**)
- FR27: A course listed as a seminar for which no theme is published is shown as a seminar with the theme marked unknown, under the FR23 flag. (**D-31**, **D-27**; 3 of 15 this term)

### Advisor

- FR28: An advisor can see their advisees with a count of each one's outstanding requirements. (**D-01**, **D-25**)
- FR29: An advisor can run the same search on a student's behalf and see what the student sees. (**D-01**, **F-04**)
- FR30: An advisor can check a student's proposed term against every outstanding requirement in one pass. (SOW §6 row 5, **F-04**)
- FR31: Depth-study candidates are proposed to an advisor for confirmation and are never asserted as fact. (**F-04**, **D-17**, SOW §6 row 6)
- FR32: The system warns when no offered section closes an outstanding requirement. (SOW §6 row 7, **F-12**, **F-13**)

### Access

- FR33: A user signs in as either a student or an advisor, and sees the view for that role. (**D-20**, **F-40**)
- FR34: A student has exactly one advisor. The student records person has no role in the system. (**D-25**, **F-39**)

## Non-Functional Requirements

### Performance

- NFR1: A phrase search returns ranked results in under 2 seconds without the reranker, over ~9,600 sections.
- NFR2: With reranking enabled, results return in under 10 seconds, or the fallback in NFR5 fires.
- NFR3: The retrieval index is built once per process, not per request.

### Reliability

- NFR4: The product functions with no network access and no API credential. Ranking degrades, it does not fail.
- NFR5: Any reranker failure — credential, network, malformed response, schema change — is logged and serves deterministic results. A graded demonstration must not depend on a round trip.
- NFR6: Concurrent reads during a long-running description fetch must not fail. (WAL plus busy timeout; measured defect, now fixed.)

### Correctness and auditability

- NFR7: Requirement tagging is rule-based and reproducible. The same inputs produce the same tags, and the rule that produced a tag can be shown.
- NFR8: No requirement may be marked satisfied by double-counting one completed course against the same requirement twice. (Measured defect: SPA 101 is both World Language and Humanities, producing 19 hours against a 9-hour pool.)
- NFR9: The rule set states which catalog year and which source document it encodes, and a rule carrying no catalog year is not displayed as authoritative. (**D-29**, **D-32**)
- NFR10: Model-produced text is labelled as such and is never the sole basis for a requirement claim.

### Privacy and compliance

- NFR11: Completed coursework is student education-record data. FERPA applies, and the DegreeWorks import path must be resolved against it before it ships. (Open — see §9.)
- NFR12: No personally identifying data leaves the machine. The off-machine payload is limited to the phrase, outstanding bucket names, and public catalog text. Asserted by test, not by comment.
- NFR13: The system stores no grades.

### Usability

- NFR14: The product informs the manual search rather than replacing it. Browsing the full list with tags remains available. (**D-04**, **F-02**)
- NFR15: Output is never pitched on user frustration, and the interface does not assume the user believes they have the problem. (**D-14**, **A-10**)
- NFR16: Every AI-produced suggestion is visibly labelled as proposed, with the text it was derived from. (**R1**, **F-04**)

### Integration

- NFR17: Course and section data come from Banner, via a scraper requiring no login. HTML-escaped text must be unescaped before attribute splitting. (Measured defect: `&amp;` ends in a semicolon and the attribute column joins on `"; "`, splitting one attribute into two; affected 220 attribute rows and 581 titles.)
- NFR18: Course descriptions are course-level, not section-level, and are cached once per subject and number. About 5% of courses have no description record in Banner.
- NFR19: Seminar theme data has no Banner source and is loaded from the term course booklet. The refresh is manual and must be documented per term. (**D-31**)
